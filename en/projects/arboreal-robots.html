Arboreal robots R&D

Robots that work in trees, not on two legs on the ground. First a shape you can see, then what can touch the bark without ruining the tree, then locomotion that actually climbs. None of this is a working farm robot, and none of it is for sale.

These pages collect the prototypes as we build them.

Prototype

First climbing shape

One of the first proofs of concept at DLAB, May 2024. Printed bioplastic, servos, firmware. Reduced mobility. You improve what you can see.

Prototype

PLA tree gripper

Can the part that touches the tree be bioplastic? Printed PLA, hobby servos. Lab at DLAB, then a trunk at StartHub, June 2024.

Prototype

Bioplastic trunk climber

Made to go up and down a trunk. Bioplastic grippers, June 2024. When the trunk branches, it cannot follow. Needs more mobility.

Mechanism

Printed gear transmission

3D-printed gears, brushed DC motor, potentiometer. Lab at DLAB, July 2024. A bench test of whether the transmission is usable on a robot.

What we are researching

  • Contact without metal on bark — metal scars, breaks, and those wounds let in disease. Conventional plastic pollutes. Bioplastic is the test: a PLA gripper, and microspines in soy-oil resin next to PLA filament.
  • Canopy locomotion — grip and motion through branches, not walking on the ground. An early trunk climber cannot switch onto branches. A tiny microspine platform gripped well but only on a certain branch size, and was too small to carry work. Gibbon Bot is the later architecture.
  • Lightweight mechanics — so the soil is not compacted and the tree is not a casualty of the clamp. That includes printed gears as a motor transmission, tested on the bench before they go on a machine.
  • Intended jobs — maintenance, harvest, hail nets, monitoring — if a machine can climb. Not there yet.
