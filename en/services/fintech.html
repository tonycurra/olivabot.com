Engineering consultancy for Amsterdam fintech

Amsterdam is Europe's fintech capital, and most of its engineering problems are the same three: something is too slow, something has to be exactly right, or customer data is somewhere it should not be. Olivabot B.V. consults on all three. Deep assessment €2,500. Builds from €5,000. All excluding VAT.

How we charge

A deep technical assessment is €2,500 excluding VAT — profile the hot path, or map where customer data actually goes, then a prioritised written plan. Project work from €5,000, in milestones. Larger jobs from €10,000. No day rate, no timesheet. Email services@olivabot.com.

What we take on

  • Latency and throughput. Profile the hot path, then say what to rewrite, what to leave alone, and what is actually a database problem wearing a code costume. C++, Rust, and the glue around them.
  • Correctness where money moves. Ledgers, reconciliation, idempotency, retries, and the edge cases that only show up at month end. Boring, unglamorous, and the reason people sleep.
  • Getting off an expensive API. A US LLM API is slow for Dutch users, priced per token forever, and ships your customers' text off the continent. We put a local model on a machine you control in the EU instead.
  • Data residency in practice. Where customer data actually goes — not where the architecture diagram says it goes. Cloud regions, backups, logs, analytics, and that one integration nobody remembers.
  • A second pair of eyes. Two days of review before you commit a quarter of engineering time to a rewrite.

What we are not

We are not a high-frequency trading shop, and if your problem is nanoseconds on an exchange feed you want a specialist — most Amsterdam prop firms hire permanent staff for that anyway. We are not your security team, not auditors, and not a law firm. We do not sell a GDPR certificate.

We are useful to payments companies, lenders, ledger and treasury teams, and fintech scale-ups that need a senior consultant for days or weeks rather than a permanent hire.

Where the data lives

This comes up in almost every fintech conversation, so it is worth being plain about it. Keeping customer data on EU infrastructure is a technical decision we can make and implement: EU regions, EU-hosted models, backups that do not quietly cross an ocean, logs that do not contain what they should not.

It is not the same as being compliant. You still own that, and a lawyer still writes the policies. We can sign a processing agreement when we hold data on your behalf, and we can run the server in the EU if you would rather not.

Why firmware people are talking about fintech

Because it is the same discipline. A trading stack and a battery-powered device both punish waste: milliseconds in one, milliwatts in the other, and in both cases the fix is knowing what the hardware is really doing. We write firmware where every cycle costs battery and control loops that cannot miss a deadline. That habit transfers directly to code that has to be fast and predictable on a server.
