How do I choose the right farm robot?

Start from the work, not a brochure.

  1. Name the job, not the machine — harvest, prune, spray, weed, or monitor.
  2. Match crop and layout — olive groves and orchards are not a flat cereal field. Slope, trunks, and canopy decide what can run.
  3. Ask what is sold this season, not what a lab promises for next year.
  4. Check batteries, cameras, and labour math. If it cannot last a shift or replace enough hours, it will not pay.
  5. Get a second view that is not selling one brand. That is this offer.

This includes harvest, weeding, spray, pruning and canopy robots, orchard and grove machines, greenhouse robots, and drones or sensors when a robot is the wrong buy.
