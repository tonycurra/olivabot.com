What this is for

Farms and agritech teams hear the same pitch: autonomous this, AI that, harvest next year. Most of it needs flat fields, a technician on site, or a crop layout that is not yours. The useful question is narrower: what is the problem, and what can solve it now?

Typical jobs we hear:

  • Not enough people for harvest, pruning, or weeding — and the window is short.
  • Work still done in the canopy: ladders, poles, walking every tree.
  • Heavy machines that compact soil or cannot enter the grove.
  • Spraying or monitoring that is either too coarse or too expensive to do often.
  • A brochure or a demo that looked fine on a different farm.

What you get

  • One video call, 45 minutes. We ask about crop, layout, terrain, labour, and the job you want done.
  • A written report (within five working days): your situation in plain language; what is worth automating now vs later vs not at all; which robot or machine categories could do the job today, with names when we know a real product; what would fail (slope, canopy, battery, sensing, economics); and what to do instead if a robot is the wrong buy.
  • Scope is one operation: one crop / site and one main job (for example harvest, prune, spray, weed, or monitor). More sites or a shopping list of six jobs is a separate quote.
