Edge AI in Amsterdam

The model works on a computer. The device cannot run it. Olivabot B.V., registered in Amsterdam, puts it on the chip: fast enough, and still inside the battery budget. The work is remote. You get a Dutch invoice.

  • What you get. A quantised model running on your microcontroller or camera module, measured on the hardware you will ship, with a short note on speed and power.
  • Time and price. About two to three weeks, €6,000–10,000. A written look first — whether it fits, how fast, what it does to the battery — is three to five days, €2,500–4,000.
  • How it starts. Half before any work. You own the files. One page of scope the day you reply.

Who this is for

Wearables, medical devices, industrial sensors, drones, and cameras in Amsterdam and the rest of the EU. Teams that already have a model and a board. If it will not fit the chip or the battery, that is the answer, before the build.

Where this has shipped

Movement classification on a finger-ring sensor for Madglove: the device itself recognises the hand. A feasibility study on on-board canopy vision. Basil Bot: a camera, a model that finds a leaf, then joint commands from that image.

The board, the radio, and the battery around that model are firmware.
